1. MODEL PURPOSE AND DESCRIPTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The ESG Text Classifier receives unstructured text from corporate sustainability disclosures - including annual reports, CDP questionnaire responses, SEC 10-K and 10-Q filings, proxy statements, and standalone ESG or sustainability reports - and assigns one or more classification labels from the following taxonomy:

Primary categories: Environmental (E), Social (S), Governance (G), Not ESG Relevant
Sub-categories (Environmental): Climate Risk, Energy Use, Water Management, Biodiversity, Waste and Circularity, Supply Chain Emissions
Sub-categories (Social): Labor Practices, Diversity and Inclusion, Community Impact, Human Rights, Customer Privacy
Sub-categories (Governance): Board Composition, Executive Compensation, Audit and Controls, Transparency and Disclosure, Shareholder Rights

For each classified passage, the model also returns: (a) a confidence score between 0.0 and 1.0, (b) a short rationale string (1–2 sentences) explaining the primary classification, and (c) a relevance weight used downstream in ESG score aggregation.

The model is implemented as a fine-tuned call to GPT-4 Turbo via Azure OpenAI Service, wrapped in a GreenScore AI proprietary prompt engineering layer. The prompt layer enforces output format, applies domain-specific classification rubrics derived from GRI Standards and SASB frameworks, and includes few-shot examples drawn from the GreenScore human-labeled dataset. A deterministic post-processing layer normalizes outputs, enforces category hierarchy, and escalates low-confidence items (below 0.72 threshold) for analyst review.

Approximately 14,000 documents are processed monthly in batch mode. A smaller real-time pathway handles urgent client requests with a target latency of under 30 seconds per document.

2. INTENDED USERS AND USE CASES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Primary Users:
- ESG Research Analysts (internal) - review and validate model outputs before publication
- Portfolio Managers (internal) - consume ESG scores derived from model outputs
- Institutional Clients (external) - receive ESG scores in client-facing reports; do not interact with model directly

Intended Use Cases:
- Batch classification of corporate disclosures for ESG scoring
- Identification of disclosure gaps (topics present/absent in company reporting)
- Trend analysis across reporting periods for the same issuer
- Input generation for the Portfolio Optimizer (GS-MDL-002)

Out-of-Scope Uses:
- Direct publication without analyst review
- Classification of personally identifiable information about individuals
- Legal or compliance determination (model outputs are informational, not legal conclusions)
- Use on audio or image content (text-only model)
- Application to languages other than English without explicit validation (see Section 5)

3. PERFORMANCE METRICS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Evaluation was conducted on a held-out test set of 2,100 passages, labeled by three independent ESG analysts with inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's Kappa = 0.81, interpreted as strong agreement). The test set was stratified to ensure geographic and sector representation.

Overall Performance (multi-label, macro-averaged):
  Precision:               0.887
  Recall:                  0.871
  F1 Score:                0.879
  AUC-ROC:                 0.934

Per-Category F1 Scores:
  Environmental (E):       0.901
  Social (S):              0.864
  Governance (G):          0.882
  Not ESG Relevant:        0.871

Sub-category F1 (selected):
  Climate Risk:            0.923
  Labor Practices:         0.848
  Board Composition:       0.895
  Human Rights:            0.811  ← below team target of 0.85; under review
  Biodiversity:            0.839  ← monitored; limited training data

Performance Thresholds and Triggers:
- Alert threshold: macro F1 drops below 0.85 on monthly shadow evaluation set
- Escalation threshold: macro F1 drops below 0.80 (triggers model review gate)
- Confidence score calibration checked monthly against analyst override rates

4. BIAS TESTING RESULTS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Bias testing was conducted as part of the Q1 2026 Bias Audit (GS-AUDIT-BIAS-2026-Q1). The following protected and sensitive attributes were tested via disparate impact analysis and counterfactual probing:

Geographic Origin of Issuer:
- North America / Western Europe (reference group): F1 0.893
- Eastern Europe / Latin America: F1 0.878; impact ratio 0.984 - PASS
- Middle East / Africa: F1 0.851; impact ratio 0.953 - PASS
- South / Southeast Asia: F1 0.829; impact ratio 0.928 - PASS (marginal; flagged for monitoring)
- Disclosure language (non-English, translated): not currently validated - OUT OF SCOPE

Sector Bias (potential source of differential performance):
- Extractive industries (mining, oil and gas): Environmental F1 0.921 - no bias concern
- Financial services issuers (disclosures are shorter, more formulaic): Overall F1 0.867 - within tolerance
- Small-cap issuers (disclosure quality lower): Overall F1 0.843 - flagged; guidance issued to analysts

Gender-Coded Language (executive reference test):
- Counterfactual probing with gender-swapped executive names showed no statistically significant classification difference (p > 0.12 across all categories) - PASS

Race / Ethnicity:
- Not directly applicable to corporate issuer classification. Counterfactual probing of company name and country of incorporation showed no systematic bias in category assignment - PASS

Overall Bias Audit Result: PASSED. One marginal finding (South/Southeast Asia geography) is being tracked in the model improvement roadmap.

5. KNOWN LIMITATIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. English-Only Validation: The model has only been formally validated on English-language documents. French, German, and Spanish documents are in scope for a multilingual evaluation planned for Q3 2026. Until then, non-English documents are translated via a third-party API before classification, and outputs are flagged for enhanced analyst review.

2. Emerging Market Disclosure Quality: Companies in developing markets often produce shorter, less structured disclosures, leading to lower recall for Social and Governance sub-categories. Clients receiving scores for these issuers are notified of this limitation in the data quality appendix of their reports.

3. Foundation Model Dependency: The model relies on GPT-4 Turbo via Azure. Changes to the underlying foundation model (version updates, deprecation) can affect performance without notice. GreenScore AI maintains a shadow evaluation pipeline that re-runs benchmark tests within 48 hours of any Azure OpenAI service version change.

4. Prompt Sensitivity: Minor changes to prompt structure can affect output consistency. The prompt layer is version-controlled (Git) and changes require AI Governance Team approval.

5. Sub-category Limitations: Human Rights and Biodiversity sub-categories have F1 scores below the team's target of 0.85. Outputs in these categories carry a mandatory analyst review flag regardless of confidence score.

6. HUMAN OVERSIGHT MECHANISMS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

- Low-confidence escalation: All outputs with confidence score < 0.72 are routed to the ESG analyst review queue within 4 hours of processing
- Sub-category flags: Human Rights and Biodiversity outputs always enter review queue regardless of confidence
- Analyst override: Any analyst may override a classification with a documented reason; overrides are logged and used in model retraining cycles
- Quality sampling: ESG Research Lead reviews a random 5% sample of accepted (non-escalated) outputs monthly to detect confidence calibration drift
- EU disclosure compliance: All client-facing reports include the AI transparency disclosure required under EU AI Act Article 52

7. MONITORING APPROACH
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Operational monitoring occurs at three levels:

Real-time: API error rates, latency (p50, p95, p99), and malformed output rates are tracked via Azure Monitor. Alerts fire to on-call ML engineer if error rate exceeds 2% over any 15-minute window.

Weekly: Shadow evaluation against a static labeled benchmark of 500 passages. Performance metrics are posted to the AI Governance Team dashboard. Any metric outside tolerance triggers a review ticket.

Monthly: Full data drift analysis comparing current month's input distribution (document length, vocabulary, sector mix, geographic mix) against the training distribution using Population Stability Index (PSI). PSI > 0.2 triggers a retraining evaluation gate.

No AI incidents have been recorded for this model since deployment (2023-08-14). One near-miss event was logged in November 2024: an Azure OpenAI service outage caused a 6-hour batch processing delay. This was handled under the standard IT incident process and did not affect client outputs. The event prompted the addition of a queue-backed retry mechanism and is documented in GS-IR-2024-003.
