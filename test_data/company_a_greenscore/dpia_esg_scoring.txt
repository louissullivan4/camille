1. PURPOSE AND CONTEXT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

This Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) has been prepared in accordance with GDPR Article 35 and GreenScore AI's DPIA Policy (GS-POL-DPIA-001). A DPIA is required because the ESG Scoring System: (a) involves systematic and large-scale processing of personal data relating to identifiable individuals named in corporate disclosures (executives, directors, named employees); (b) uses automated processing including AI (GS-MDL-001) that produces evaluative outputs used in client-facing reports; and (c) may involve cross-border transfers to EU client data environments.

This DPIA covers:
- The ESG Text Classifier (GS-MDL-001) and its input processing pipeline
- The ESG score aggregation and storage systems
- The client-facing ESG report generation and delivery process
- Cross-border data flows associated with EU client delivery

This DPIA does not cover the Portfolio Optimizer (GS-MDL-002) or Risk Predictor (GS-MDL-003), which are the subjects of separate DPIAs (GS-DPIA-002 and GS-DPIA-003 respectively).

2.1 Input Data — Corporate Disclosure Documents

The primary inputs to the ESG Scoring System are corporate disclosure documents. These are predominantly documents about corporate entities (organizational data) rather than about individuals. However, these documents frequently reference identified individuals, including:
- Named corporate executives (CEO, CFO, board directors) referenced in governance sections
- Named employees mentioned in labor practice or diversity disclosures
- Named individuals in community impact or human rights-related disclosures
- Individuals named in controversy reports or litigation summaries

Categories of personal data that may be incidentally processed:
- Names and professional roles of named executives and directors (publicly disclosed)
- Statements about named individuals' conduct, decisions, or characteristics
- Salary and compensation data for named senior individuals (disclosed in annual reports)
- Demographic information (gender, nationality) disclosed voluntarily in ESG reports

Legal basis for processing: GDPR Article 6(1)(f) — Legitimate interests. GreenScore AI's legitimate interest: providing ESG investment research and scoring services to institutional clients. A Legitimate Interests Assessment (LIA) is documented in GS-LIA-ESG-001 and concluded that this interest is not overridden by data subjects' interests given: (a) the data subjects are public figures in their professional capacity; (b) the information processed is already publicly disclosed; (c) the processing does not involve special category data; and (d) appropriate safeguards are in place.

Special Category Data: The ESG Scoring System does not intentionally process special category data (GDPR Article 9). Demographic information (e.g., board gender diversity statistics) is processed at an aggregate level in the context of corporate governance assessment, not at the level of identified individuals. Any incidental appearance of special category data is flagged by the data quality pipeline for human review.

2.2 Third-Party Processing — Azure OpenAI

GS-MDL-001 is implemented via Azure OpenAI Service (Microsoft). When document text containing personal data is submitted for classification, that text is transmitted to Microsoft Azure datacenters for processing. This constitutes a data processor relationship.

Data processing agreement: GreenScore AI has executed Microsoft's Data Processing Addendum (DPA) for Azure OpenAI Service. Under this DPA: (a) Microsoft processes data only as instructed by GreenScore AI; (b) Microsoft does not use customer data to train its models; (c) Microsoft implements appropriate technical and organizational security measures; (d) Microsoft provides audit cooperation on reasonable request.

2.3 Output Data and Storage

ESG classification outputs: Stored in GreenScore AI's internal data lake (AWS S3, EU-West region). Contains: document reference ID, passage text (the source material), classification labels, confidence scores, analyst review records, and timestamps. Retention: 5 years (see Section 6).

ESG aggregate scores: Company-level ESG scores aggregated from classification outputs. Stored in the ESG Score Database (PostgreSQL on AWS RDS, EU-West region). Retention: 5 years.

Client reports: PDF reports delivered to clients containing company-level ESG scores and narrative commentary. Do not contain identified individual personal data at the level of a natural person in normal circumstances. Retention: 7 years (MiFID II record-keeping requirement).

4. DATA MINIMIZATION MEASURES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The following measures are in place to comply with GDPR Article 5(1)(c) (data minimization):

1. Passage-Level Processing: Documents are segmented into passages before submission to the classifier. Only passages flagged as potentially ESG-relevant by a lightweight pre-filter are sent to the Azure OpenAI API. This reduces the volume of personal data transmitted.

2. No Individual Profiling: The system is designed to score corporate entities, not individuals. ESG scores are issued at the company level. Individual-level personal data is processed only to the extent it appears incidentally in the corporate disclosure text; no individual-level profiles or scores are produced.

3. Personal Data Masking (Under Development): The AI Governance Team is developing a pre-processing step to pseudonymize named individuals in source texts before transmission to Azure OpenAI where the individual is not relevant to the ESG classification. Target deployment: Q4 2026. Until deployed, the Azure DPA and prompt design serve as the primary safeguards.

4. Source Document Retention Review: GreenScore AI retains raw source documents for 2 years (see Section 6). Source documents obtained from third-party licensed data providers are subject to those providers' deletion and rights provisions.

6. CROSS-BORDER TRANSFER MECHANISMS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

GreenScore AI processes personal data of EU data subjects and delivers ESG reports to EU-based institutional clients. The following transfer mechanisms are in place:

Transfer 1 — GreenScore AI (UK) → Azure OpenAI (Microsoft EU Region):
Data is processed in Microsoft's EU-West Azure region (Ireland/Netherlands). GreenScore AI's Azure tenancy is configured to restrict processing to EU regions. No transfer outside the EU/EEA occurs for this pathway. Transfer mechanism: not required (data remains within EEA).

Transfer 2 — GreenScore AI (UK, post-Brexit) → EU Clients:
The UK is not subject to an EU adequacy decision for all purposes. However, GreenScore AI's contractual data sharing with EU institutional clients is governed by Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) per EU Commission Decision 2021/914 (Module 1: Controller-to-Controller). SCCs are incorporated into the Master Service Agreement signed with each EU client. Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) has been conducted and concluded that UK law does not present disproportionate risk to EU data subjects given the nature of the data (primarily organizational, not sensitive).

Transfer 3 — GreenScore AI → MSCI / Bloomberg / Refinitiv (licensed data providers):
These relationships involve GreenScore AI receiving data from third parties (not transferring EU personal data out). Data Processing Agreements with each provider are maintained by the Legal team.

8. DATA SUBJECT RIGHTS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Individuals named in processed documents may exercise GDPR data subject rights by contacting dpo@greenscore.ai. GreenScore AI acknowledges the right to access, rectification, erasure (subject to legal retention obligations), restriction of processing, and objection to processing on legitimate interests grounds.

In practice, most personal data processed consists of publicly disclosed information about individuals in their professional corporate roles. GreenScore AI's position (documented in the LIA) is that legitimate interests override individual objection rights for this specific processing, given the public interest in ESG transparency and the data subjects' status as public corporate officials.
